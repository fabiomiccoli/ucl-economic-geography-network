Seminar abstract: The relationship between regional specialisation and economic performance is a contested issue. Although from a theoretical viewpoint it is often argued that specialisation improves regional competitiveness,
 the empirical evidence so far is particularly thin. The few studies that have empirically investigated the issue predominantly focus on industrial/manufacturing sectors. Furthermore, they find limited evidence that specialisation in particular sectors is the
 driving force behind better regional outcomes. The present study explores the extent to which industrial structure affected the labour productivity performance of 45 ITL 2 (International Territorial Level 2) spatial units in Great Britain during the period
 2015-2023, by analysing data for 72 two-digit level SIC (Standard Industrial Classification) sectors covering the entire spectrum of economic activity. This allows to account for the diversity of modern, service-based, regional economies and also to consider
 whether spatial policies based on the promotion of specialisation are likely to be effective in addressing the wide and persistent inequalities in labour productivity.
